01. The problem

People don't read, they calculate.

Be's lending KPI needed to grow sixfold in a year, with Cash Loan as the main driver. The old page explained the product like a bank: a wall of text and a button.

KPI target

6×

growth needed year over year, with Cash Loan as the main driver of GMV

Submission rate

4.69–7.02%

of users who tapped in went on to submit, despite real intent to borrow

How might we help users understand and trust the quick loan service from the first view, to increase the conversion rate from onboarding view to loan registration?

02. Research

Everyone leads with the calculator, not the theory.

A teardown of Momo, ZaloPay and ShopeePay showed one pattern: a loan calculator up front, no theory. The heatmap agreed: 20% of clicks went to the calculator, while text-heavy sections pulled only 6–7%.

#Step 1: Competitor teardown

What works

Low cognitive load, a transparent loan model and testimonials that build credibility.

What is missing

First-time borrowers still need an explanation and onboarding to understand the product.

Shared pattern

Rate and limit sit at the top, with an early loan-amount step. The revamp features the rate and moves the calculator into the flow.

#Step 2: Click data

The user demand behind these decisions

Confirmed by triangulating competitor benchmark, PO insight, and revamp data.

Where clicks actually went

Page text 6–7%

🔵 User demand

The actual cost felt scarier than it was.

🔵 User demand

03. Key objective

Three Things the Page Had to Do

Objective: Increase the conversion rate from the onboarding screen to loan registration.

01.

Calculator First, Not Last

Replace a page built for reading with a page built for answering the one question everyone actually has: how much, and what do I pay.

02.

Rate Before Limit

Help individuals grasp the true costs before they dive deeper into the process—fewer applications, but those that are more likely to convert and gain approval.

03.

Clear communication

Frame the true cost, and make it as clear as possible so it feels approachable without hiding any part of it because in lending, trust is the product.

04. Architecture

Rebuilt around cost, trust and steps.

The old page was a brochure: hero, heading, checklist, consent, CTA. The new one answers questions in the order users ask them.

05. Design

Three ways to put the numbers first.

I proposed three layouts that all front-load the cost. They differ in what leads: the calculator, the limit or the rate.

2. Internal review and A/B testing of the options

After presenting 3 options, the team decided to conduct internal A/B testing, comparing the current version (old) with all 3 newly proposed options. After one week with 20–30 users, Option C went to 100% of users: it builds trust first, then offers the calculator as the closing step.

📉 final result

After 1 week of A/B testing with 20 – 30 users, the team decided to roll out Option C to 100% of users - because the interface of Option C informs users what the product is, why it is trustworthy first, and then presents the loan calculator as a "closing" step.

Results after going into production:
The loan registration click-through rate increased from 48% to 58%.

3. Final design

Option C became the new onboarding page. Each note explains why a block sits where it does, and how it builds the motivation to apply.

4. Using the loan calculator

Tapping “View loan illustration” in the hero opens this sheet in place, so users can test the numbers without leaving the page. The notes follow the order of use.

08. Reflection

What I learned.

  1. 01

    Lead with the number users came for

    The page won when cost, not brand, led. Next time I would start from the user's main question, not the product story.

  2. 02

    Test the order, not just the visuals

    Options A, B and C used the same ingredients. The sequence made the difference, so I would test sequence earlier.
