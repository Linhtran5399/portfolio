# Design Brief — Linh Tran Portfolio

_Generated by /design-grill on 2026-11-05. Update this file when design decisions change._

## Product Context
- **Category:** Marketing site — personal portfolio, hiring-focused (static HTML/CSS, built from the Figma file "Portfolio")
- **Target user:** Hiring decision-makers and their influencers: design managers, heads of product, product managers, recruiters, founders, tech leads and principal engineers, in Vietnam, SEA and Europe. When roles conflict, design for the design manager / head of product.
- **Primary device/context:** Desktop for now. Skimming 10–15 candidate tabs under time pressure; ~60 seconds per portfolio; looking for what matters (impact, role, craft).
- **Defining screen:** Work home (`index.html`) — hero, bento intro, work cards. Bar for "perfect": within 5 seconds the reader knows who Linh is, what Linh does, and sees at least one measurable outcome.

## Brand Personality
**5 adjectives:** confident, precise, direct, credible, thoughtful
**Emotional goal:** Confident ("I'd bet on this person") — plus "a joy to work with"
**Tone:** The Economist register: authoritative, restrained, evidence-led, with serif headlines doing the editorial work. Authority lives in the content and hierarchy, not in a cold look. Joy comes through plain, human voice and small details, not loud visuals.

## Visual References
- **Ben Shih** (benshih.design) — voice and mindset: candid, first-person, shows how the problem was framed; Impact Overview up front, role/team/timeline metadata, metric under each before/after.
- **Rachel Chen** (rachelchen.tech) — voice and mindset: casual, personal, "now" role list.
- **Navyaa Taneja** (navyaataneja.com) — UI visual: warm, image-led, numbered sections, an emphasised accent phrase in headings, small playful touches.
- **Rachel Tan** (rachelatwork.com) — UI visual: full-width work cards with the outcome in the title.

_Note: references were read as text only; their fonts and colours were not verified._

## What to Avoid
- Looks like: template-looking portfolios (Notion/Framer/Webflow templates) — identical card grids, generic hero, no point of view.
- Looks like: pretty-but-empty portfolios — heavy visuals and animation, no outcomes, no "what I did vs what the team did."
- Looks like: Dribbble-style showcases — polished UI shots with no problem framing.
- Differentiate with: outcome-first work cards, explicit "my role" in each case study, serif + accent-blue editorial touch.

## Color System
- **Default mode:** Light (light only)
- **Temperature:** Neutral surfaces with a cool accent; neutrals kept gently warm to soften the blue
- **Primary color direction:** confident blue — `--core-primary`
- **Palette approach:** Single brand colour + neutrals. Pastel tags (orange/blue/purple) and bento green are small supporting accents only, never a second brand colour.
- **Saturation:** Moderately saturated accent; muted pastels elsewhere
- **Specific starting point (current tokens):** primary: #4770f1, background: #ffffff, card: #f8f8fa, text: #212529, border: #e5e5e1

## Typography
- **Typeface:** Playfair Display (headings) + Inter (body) — editorial voice for headlines, fast-to-skim body
- **Scale approach:** Spacious between sections, compact inside cards. **Current scale stays as is** (body 14px, no resizing).
- **Heading/body:** Display + text pair. Playfair for hero and section headings only, with an italic/accent phrase as the signature touch; Inter for nav, cards, metrics.
- **Suggested pair:** Playfair Display (headings at 600–700) + Inter (body at 400–500)

## Component Style
- **Corner radius:** Rounded — 12px cards, 16px bento, pill CTAs and tags
- **Elevation:** Flat — card tint and borders, no drop shadows
- **Density:** Balanced
- **Borders:** Light — 1px hairline (#e5e5e1 / #e1e1e5) where a divider is needed
- **Overall:** Rounded, flat, balanced, light borders — friendly shapes with a disciplined, editorial finish.

## Animation
- **Level:** Subtle
- **Key interaction to make feel great:** Work card → case study (card hover and click-through)
- **Easing preference:** ease-out entrances, 100–200ms transitions, no bouncing or springs, no scroll-jacking or parallax. `prefers-reduced-motion` is already respected and must stay.

## Dark Mode
- Not needed

## Decisions Made
| Decision | What was decided | Why |
|----------|-----------------|-----|
| Audience | Mixed hiring audience; design for the design manager / head of product when roles conflict | They decide the shortlist; the same fast, clear signal serves recruiters and engineers |
| Register | The Economist | Authoritative, evidence-led; fits the existing serif headline |
| Emotional goal | Confident + "a joy to work with" | Joy via voice and small details, not loud visuals |
| Voice vs visuals | Voice from Ben Shih / Rachel Chen; UI from Navyaa Taneja / Rachel Tan | Resolves Economist-vs-warm-references contradiction |
| Colour | Light, single blue accent, neutrals, small pastel accents | Keeps discipline while staying warm |
| Typography | Playfair + Inter, two fonts | Already in use; editorial headings, easy body |
| Components | Rounded, flat, balanced, light borders | Matches existing radii and borders |
| Animation | Subtle; polish Work card → case study | Skimmers need confirmation, not delay |
| Defining screen | Work home | First impression; sets the system for case studies |

## Ruled Out
_Directions explicitly rejected during this session. Do not re-propose these without reopening the decision._

| Direction | Why it was rejected |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Dark mode | Not needed; image-led assets are designed on light backgrounds |
| Changing the font-size scale (e.g. body 14px → 16px) | Owner wants the current sizes kept |
| Second brand colour / full palette | Single accent is intentional; pastels stay supporting only |
| Heavy motion (parallax, scroll-driven, entrance delays) | Skimmers under time pressure; conflicts with the Economist register |
| Drop shadows / elevated cards | Flat, restrained look is intentional |
